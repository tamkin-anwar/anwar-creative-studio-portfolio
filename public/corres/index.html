How Corres reads your mail

The few emails that matter, first.

Most email asks nothing of you. Corres finds the ones that do, says why, and lets the rest step back.

Needs You

Opens on what needs you.

The emails that ask something of you come first, each with the reason it’s there. Who still owes you a reply sits one row above. Everything else waits in Primary until you get to it.

  • Waiting: replies you’re owed, with a nudge when they’re late
  • Today’s Brief, if you want it: what’s due, today’s events and deliveries on one card

Mail

A Primary inbox that’s actually primary.

People and what’s urgent up top. Everything, Promotions, Social and Updates beside it, sorted by Corres’s own rules for every account you add. Move one email, or everything from a sender, and Corres learns, and shows you what it learned.

  • A security code or an appointment never hides in Updates
  • First emails from new senders can wait for your yes
  • Swipes, lists and the toolbar under an email are yours to set

Notifications

Told when it matters, and only then.

New Outlook mail notifies within seconds, even with Corres closed. iCloud and other email can too, once you turn on Instant Notifications for that account. You choose, per account, whether that’s all new mail, people and time-sensitive mail, or only what needs you.

  • Archive, mark read or reply right from the notification
  • Newsletters can arrive silently, and VIPs always come through
  • A Morning Brief notification at the time you pick

And everything you’d expect

Complete, down to the details.

Built on Postwright

Corres runs on its own mail engine, on your device. Archive and delete happen the moment you swipe, even with no signal, and an email you send offline goes by itself once you’re back. What you do on the web or another device shows up here.

Writing, finished

Drafts that wait for you, bold, lists and links, sending from your other addresses, templates, and a check for a forgotten attachment.

Send Later & Undo Send

Schedule an email, get a reminder if nobody replies, and take back what you just sent, on iPhone, iPad and Mac.

Summaries & replies

With Apple Intelligence, long threads are summarized and checked against the email itself, and replies are drafted, all on your device. Without it, Needs You, its reasons and the key details still work.

Search, or ask

Search every account at once, archived mail included, with filters like from: and has:attachment. Or ask, “When is my flight?”, and get the answer from your own email, with the messages it came from.

New senders

Choose how first emails arrive: review everyone, review only companies, mark them new, or let everyone in. Blocking hides them in Corres and never changes your account.

Invitations & Out of Office

See when it is in your time zone, where, the call to join and whether you’re free. Accept, Maybe or Decline right in the email, and turn your automatic reply on without leaving Corres.

Rules & mute

Sort mail as it arrives. Rules can also run inside Gmail and Outlook, so they work with Corres closed. Mute a conversation so new replies stay quiet.

The details, up top

A receipt’s or bill’s amount and status, a delivery’s date, and what a long email asks of you, read from the email and shown at the top.

No tracking pixels

Remote images and read-receipt trackers are blocked until you choose to show them. Unsubscribe is one tap.

Company logos, privately

The logo a company publishes for email, shown only when a certificate backs it, and fetched by our server so no company learns when you read.

Siri, Shortcuts & widgets

Ask Siri to write, send or find an email. Needs You in widgets, Control Center and on your watch face, and deliveries and Send Later on the Lock Screen.

Made for everywhere

English, Japanese, Chinese, German, Korean, French, Spanish, Portuguese and Bengali. Deadlines, receipts, deliveries, codes and quoted replies are understood in each, and weekends follow your region.

Made for everyone

Checked with Apple’s accessibility audit. VoiceOver says what just happened, Voice Control knows what you call things, and text grows to the largest sizes on iPhone and iPad, and to twice the size on the Mac.

Replies that sound like you

Suggested replies use the greeting, sign-off and length you use, learned on your device from email you’ve sent.

Remind Me

Add any email to Reminders for this evening, tomorrow morning or when it’s due, with a link straight back to it.

Share to Corres

Share a page or photos from any app and write the email right in the Share sheet.

On Apple Watch

What needs you, and the start of each email. Reply by voice, Scribble or a ready answer, or mark it handled.

Fast with a big mailbox

Corres opens on your newest mail right away, even with thousands of conversations, and pull to refresh finishes in seconds.

Private by design

Your email is read on your own devices. Not on our servers, and never by an AI company.

Stored on your device

Mail comes straight from your email provider to your iPhone, iPad or Mac and stays there, protected by Apple’s built-in encryption. Corres’s servers never receive it.

Sorted on your device

Needs You, summaries, suggested replies and answers in Search all run on your device. Nothing is sent to an AI service or used to train one.

Nothing to sell

No ads, no analytics, no tracking, no read receipts. Corres is paid for by the people who use it.

Three small servers help, and none of them reads your email: a notification relay that learns only that a mailbox changed; a logo server that sees only the sender’s web domain; and, only if you turn on Instant Notifications for iCloud or another account that signs in with a password, a mail watcher that keeps an encrypted copy of that password and watches only for new mail. The privacy policy lists exactly what each one keeps. Corres’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Questions

Which email accounts work with Corres?

Gmail and Google Workspace, Outlook.com, Hotmail and Live, work or school Microsoft 365, iCloud Mail, and almost any other email that offers IMAP: Yahoo, AOL, Fastmail, Zoho, GMX, Yahoo! JAPAN, Naver, QQ, 163 and more. Add as many as you like; they share one inbox. iCloud and most others ask for an app password instead of your main password, and Corres walks you through making one.

Can I use my Gmail account in the beta?

Yes, once we add you. Until Google finishes reviewing Corres, Gmail sign-in works only for addresses on Google’s test list. Email us the Gmail address you’d like to use. Outlook, iCloud and other email work right away.

Does Corres read my email on its servers?

No. Your mail is downloaded from your email provider directly to your iPhone, iPad or Mac and processed there. A notification relay learns only that your mailbox changed, never what’s in it. The privacy policy has the details.

Do I need Apple Intelligence?

No. Needs You, Waiting, sorting and key details work on every supported iPhone, iPad and Mac. Summaries and drafted replies use Apple Intelligence where your device has it, on the device itself.

Is there an iPad, Mac or Apple Watch version?

Yes. Three columns on iPad, a native Mac app with its own windows, menus and keyboard shortcuts, and on Apple Watch what needs you, with replies by voice. One Corres Pro covers iPhone, iPad and Mac, and the accounts you add follow you through iCloud Keychain.

Does it work without a signal?

Yes. Your mail is on your device, so it reads offline. What you archive, delete, flag or send goes through by itself once you’re back online.

What is Postwright?

Postwright is the mail engine we built for Corres. It runs on your device and talks to Gmail, Outlook, iCloud and other mail servers each in its own way, so your actions are instant and every account stays in step with the web. It’s why Corres doesn’t need a server of its own to hold your mail.

How do I get it?

Install Apple’s TestFlight app, then open the Corres beta link on your iPhone, iPad or Mac. You can explore every screen with sample mail before adding an account. It’s coming to the App Store.
