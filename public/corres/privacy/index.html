Corres

Privacy Policy

Corres reads and sorts your email on your iPhone, iPad and Mac. It doesn't sell your data, show ads, or send your mail to an AI company.

Effective October 28, 2026. Corres is made by Anwar Creative Studio (“we”).

What Corres accesses

When you connect a Gmail account, you sign in with Google directly; Corres never sees your password. Google gives Corres permission to read, send and organize your Gmail (archive, label, star, mark read or unread, move to Trash, report spam, and save drafts), which is what an email app needs to work. Corres asks Google for one more permission, to change your Gmail settings, only when you use a feature that needs it: Out of Office, to turn your Gmail vacation reply on and off, or Rules, if you choose to also save a rule as a Gmail filter so it runs when Corres is closed. It's never asked for at sign-in. You can revoke any of this anytime in your Google Account settings. Removing the account from all your devices in Corres also withdraws Corres’s access with Google.

When you connect an Outlook account (Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live, or a work or school Microsoft 365 account), you sign in with Microsoft directly; Corres never sees your password. Microsoft gives Corres permission to read and organize your mail (archive, move, flag, mark read, categories), send mail as you, change your mailbox settings so Out of Office can turn your automatic replies on and off, and read your basic profile (your name and photo, shown in Corres). You can revoke this anytime at account.microsoft.com under Privacy → Apps and services or through your organization's IT for a work account. Removing the account in Corres deletes its sign-in from your devices; to withdraw Corres’s permission itself, use account.microsoft.com or ask your IT.

When you connect iCloud Mail, Apple asks other mail apps for an app-specific password instead of your Apple Account password. You make one at account.apple.com and give it to Corres, which checks it with iCloud and keeps it in your device's Keychain. Corres connects to iCloud's mail servers directly with it to read, organize and send your mail, and to file a copy of what you send in Sent. Corres never sees your Apple Account password. You can turn the app-specific password off anytime at account.apple.com, or remove the account in Corres.

When you connect another email account (for example Yahoo, AOL, Fastmail, Zoho, GMX, Yahoo! JAPAN, Naver, QQ, 163 or a work server), you give Corres that account's password or app password. Corres keeps it in your device's Keychain and uses it only to connect to that service's own mail servers directly, to read, organize and send your mail. For an address on its own domain, Corres finds the right mail servers by looking the domain up with Cloudflare’s DNS service and Mozilla’s public mail settings database. Only the domain is sent, never your address or password. You can change the password or turn the app password off with your provider at any time, or remove the account in Corres.

If you allow it, Corres also reads your Contacts, on your device only, to suggest people as you type and show their photos. It never uploads them. If you tap Check Your Calendar on an invitation, or add an event or a reminder from an email, Corres uses your Calendar or Reminders on your device for that; nothing from them is sent to us.

Where your mail is processed

  • On your device. Your messages are downloaded from your email provider (Google, Microsoft, Apple or another service) to your iPhone, iPad or Mac and stored there, protected by Apple’s built-in encryption. Sorting, summaries, suggested replies, rewrites, dictation and answers to questions in Search all run on your device using Apple Intelligence and Apple's on-device speech recognition.
  • Never on our servers, never with AI companies. Corres never sends the contents of your email to Anwar Creative Studio or to any AI provider. If you use Siri or Apple Intelligence with your mail, Apple handles that under its own privacy policy.
  • Your sign-in permission (or, for iCloud and other email, your password or app password) is kept in your device's Keychain, shared only with Corres's own extensions, such as the Share sheet and new-mail notifications.
  • New-mail notifications are written on your device. When a notification arrives, Corres's notification extension fetches that one email from your email provider with your own sign-in and shows its sender, subject and opening words. Nothing about the email passes through our servers.
  • Your account settings (each account's name, color, signatures and notification choices) are kept on your device, and your signatures and settings also sync to your other devices through your own iCloud (below).
  • What Corres learns from you (where you move a sender's mail, what you mark as needing no action, your habits) is kept on your device, and you can see and forget any of it in Settings.
  • Your accounts on your other devices. So an account you add on one device is ready on your others, its sign-in (a token, or an app-specific password) is kept in your iCloud Keychain, which Apple encrypts end to end so that not even Apple can read it, with a list of which accounts you have. We never receive it. If iCloud Keychain is off, it stays on the device you added it on. When you remove an account, you choose: from this device only, or from all your devices.
  • Your Corres choices on your other devices. So your iPhone, iPad and Mac agree, Corres keeps a small record of what you decide in it in your own private iCloud storage, which Apple holds for your Apple Account: conversations you moved, snoozed or pinned (by their identifiers, not their contents), senders you allowed or blocked, your VIPs, what Corres has learned, your signatures and your settings. No email content is stored there, and we can't read it. Signing out of iCloud on a device stops it.
  • Changes made without a signal (archive, delete, flag, mark read, and email you send) wait on your device and go to your email provider once you're back online.
  • Send Later keeps the email on your device and sends it from there at the time you chose. It never waits on our servers.
  • Diagnostics, only if you send them. If you tap Send Diagnostics, Corres shares what it recorded about a freeze or crash (the build, the system version and device model, where it stopped, the app’s last few actions and their timings, recent error messages, which can include a server’s reply such as an address it rejected, and Apple’s performance figures for Corres on that device) through the share sheet, to wherever you choose to send it. Never your mail.

What our servers see

  • New-mail notifications (optional). If you turn on notifications, a small relay stores your device's notification token and the email addresses of your connected accounts, so it can tell your device that something changed. Google tells the relay only that a mailbox changed. For an Outlook account, Microsoft tells it that a message arrived, changed or was deleted, and gives that message's identifier, which the relay passes straight to your device so it can fetch the email itself; the relay keeps the Outlook subscription's identifier and a scrambled (hashed) copy of the secret Corres uses to confirm notifications really come from Microsoft. The relay never receives a subject, sender or message. Turning notifications off or removing an account deletes these records, and a device that hasn’t checked in with the relay for 30 days has its records deleted automatically.
  • Instant Notifications for iCloud and other email (optional, off by default). If you turn it on for an iCloud account, or another account that signs in with a password, your iPhone seals that account's password or app password so that only Corres's mail watcher can open it, and sends it to our server with the account's address, mail server and user name. The watcher uses it to keep a connection to your Inbox and tells your iPhone when new mail arrives; your iPhone then fetches and shows the email itself. The watcher never reads, downloads or stores your email. Turning the switch off or removing the account deletes it, as does that device not checking in with our server for 30 days, and revoking the password with your provider (for iCloud, at account.apple.com) stops it working. Without it, Corres checks from your device whenever iOS gives it time.
  • Company logos (optional, on unless you turn it off). To show a company’s logo beside its email, Corres sends our logo server the web domain the email came from (for example, email.example.com). Never your address, the message or anything in it. The server fetches the logo the company publishes for email, only one checked by its certificate, so the company sees our server, never you. The server keeps no record of which domains it was asked about; logos are kept on your device. Turn them off in Settings → Private by Design.
  • Checking that requests come from Corres. On iPhone and iPad, Corres uses Apple’s App Attest so our notification relay can tell that a request comes from a genuine copy of Corres. The relay keeps the key’s identifier and public key, a counter, Apple’s attestation receipt, and whether each request passed. These identify that copy of the app, not you, and contain nothing from your email. On the Mac, Corres sends Apple’s DeviceCheck token instead; the relay checks it with Apple and keeps a one-way hash of it for a day so it can’t be reused.
  • Server logs. Like most web services, our servers’ host, Google Cloud, records each request’s time, which address was called and the caller’s IP address, and keeps these logs for 30 days for security and troubleshooting. They never contain your email or your addresses.

What we don't do

  • No advertising, no analytics or tracking SDKs, no selling or sharing of your data.
  • No read receipts: Corres never tells anyone you opened their email. Remote images and tracking pixels in email are blocked until you choose to show them.
  • Your email is never used to train AI models, ours or anyone else's.

Google user data

Corres's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Corres uses Google user data only to show you your Gmail and to carry out what you do with it in the app.

Who we share Google user data with

We don't sell Google user data, and we don't share, transfer or disclose it to advertisers, data brokers, AI companies or other apps. Your Gmail messages travel only between Google and your own devices. Our servers never receive them, so there is no Gmail content for us to share. Google user data leaves your device only in these cases:

  • You send or forward an email, which goes through Gmail to the people you address it to.
  • You choose to share something from an email with another app through the share sheet.
  • You turn on new-mail notifications. Our relay then keeps your Gmail address and receives Google’s notice that your mailbox changed (with no message, subject or sender in it), and wakes your device through Apple’s push notification service; that wake-up carries your Gmail address and nothing from your email. Our relay runs on Google Cloud, which processes this for us under its terms.
  • Company logos are on (Settings → Private by Design). To show a company’s logo, Corres sends our logo server the web domain an email came from, for example email.example.com. Never your address or anything in the email. Turn logos off and nothing is sent.
  • You use iCloud. Your sign-in token goes to your own iCloud Keychain, which Apple encrypts end to end, so your account is ready on your other devices. Your choices in Corres (senders you allowed or blocked, your VIPs, what Corres has learned, and which conversations you moved, snoozed or pinned, by their identifiers) go to your own private iCloud storage. Apple holds both for your Apple Account; we can’t read them.
  • The law requires it. We would disclose the little we hold (your Gmail address and notification records) only if legally required, and we hold no email content.

How we protect your data

  • Encrypted in transit. Every connection, between your device and Google and between your device and our relay, uses HTTPS (TLS).
  • Encrypted on your device. Your mail is stored inside Corres’s own private storage on your iPhone, iPad or Mac, which Apple encrypts and other apps can’t read.
  • Sign-ins in the Keychain. Your Google sign-in token is kept in your device’s Keychain, Apple’s encrypted store for passwords and keys, and in your iCloud Keychain only if you use it, which Apple encrypts end to end. Corres never sees your Google password.
  • Little on our servers, and not for long. Our relay keeps only what notifications need (above). Google Cloud encrypts it at rest, each service runs with only the permissions it needs, and a device’s records are deleted when you turn notifications off or remove the account, or after the device hasn’t checked in with the relay for 30 days.
  • Checking for genuine copies of Corres. The relay checks Apple’s App Attest (iPhone and iPad) or DeviceCheck (Mac) to tell genuine copies of Corres from other software, and we are moving it to refuse requests that fail the check.
  • Your control. You can remove Corres’s access anytime at myaccount.google.com/connections. Removing a Gmail account from all your devices in Corres also asks Google to withdraw its access and deletes what our relay held for it.

Purchases

Corres Pro is sold through the App Store. Apple handles payment; we receive no card details. Your plan is verified on your device from Apple's signed receipts.

Deleting your data

Remove an account in Corres (Settings → the account → Remove Account). From this device: its mail, settings and this device’s notification records on our servers, including a sealed password held for Instant Notifications, are deleted. From all devices: the same happens on your other devices as each one opens, or within 30 days, and for Gmail, Corres asks Google to withdraw its access. Deleting the app removes everything stored on your device. Nothing is deleted from your email account itself.

Children

Corres is not directed to children under 13.
